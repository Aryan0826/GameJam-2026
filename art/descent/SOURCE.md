# Forward flight footage and centered cockpit

No AI generated images or video are used.

## Star flight animation

**StarfieldSimulation.gif** by **Jahobr**, April 20, 2017.
The creator publishes the MATLAB source code used to render the animation.

Source and original animation: https://commons.wikimedia.org/wiki/File:StarfieldSimulation.gif
Selected license: **CC0 1.0 Universal**.
License: https://creativecommons.org/publicdomain/zero/1.0/

`star-flight.ogv` converts the existing 500 × 400 animation to 1000 × 800 Ogg Theora at 30 fps, looped to approximately 8.067 seconds without audio. Stars move outwards from a single vanishing point as the camera moves forward. This is conventional computer animation, not actual spaceflight footage. No new stars were generated with AI.

The runtime video is positioned with its vanishing point at (660, 330) in the 1320 × 850 cockpit view. There is no camera roll or lateral flight, and both planets use neutral white stars on black space.

## Centered cockpit photograph

**Space Shuttle Atlantis OV-104 main flight deck.png** by **DMolybdenum / Daniel Molybdenum**, December 8, 2019.

Source: https://commons.wikimedia.org/wiki/File:Space_Shuttle_Atlantis_OV-104_main_flight_deck.png
Original author page: https://www.flickr.com/photos/165445115@N07/49188085087/
Public domain status: the author marked this photograph as a Public Domain Work on Flickr; Wikimedia Commons records a reviewed public domain release.
Public Domain Mark: https://creativecommons.org/publicdomain/mark/1.0/

`cockpit-frame.png` is an ordinary photographic edit of the panorama. It is reprojected to a centered perspective facing forward, using a 75 degree horizontal field of view, panorama longitude fraction 0.58, and 3 degree upward pitch. It is darkened, desaturated, resized to 1320 × 850, and window areas are manually masked to transparency. The star animation plays only behind these window openings. Controls and cockpit remain fixed on screen. At the end, an incoming envoy hologram rises from the console, expands, and reveals the conversation screen. The interface transition uses Godot primitives and the already credited CC0 character sprites.

The previous angled cockpit and Earth reentry clip are no longer used. The sequence plays locally and does not need an internet connection.
